Hypothesis assessed

A multi-agent LLM validation layer—a prosecutor, a defender, and judges—applied to STGP-evolved 0DTE strategies reduces realized losses measured by 5% Conditional Value at Risk and maximum drawdown, at 95% confidence, compared with deploy-all, rule-based selection, and a single LLM.

The 5% specifies the CVaR tail fraction; 95% specifies the confidence level. The hypothesis does not specify a minimum percentage reduction.

Allocation rule

WONT_GENERALIZE receives zero allocation. Otherwise, allocation decreases monotonically with risk score:

size = (100 − risk score) / 100

A risk score of 40 produces size 0.60, or 60% of one full-size strategy.

The court uses the panel’s median risk score; the single LLM uses its own risk score. The court funds 40 strategies and the single LLM funds 57.

How the baselines are made comparable

  • Discard no strategies: deploy-all funds all 57 champions at full size, without validation.
  • Evidence of skill: rank all champions by net training return and assign the court’s exact allocation sizes from largest to smallest along that ranking. This preserves the allocation multiset, funded count, and total nominal allocation. Individual court scores and held-out returns do not determine the rule’s ranking. Training-return ties are resolved by champion ID.
  • Single LLM: use the same score-only formula and WONT veto as the court, with the single model’s own risk scores and verdicts.

All policies use the same 57 champions and 882 dated held-out observations (2022-09-19–2026-04-09). Zero-allocation days remain on the calendar. The evidence-of-skill control uses the court’s aggregate sizing schedule, but chooses where to place those sizes solely from training returns. No new LLM calls or fitting on test outcomes were used.

H3 criterion: for each specified baseline, the 95% confidence intervals for CVaR improvement and maximum-drawdown reduction must both lie entirely above zero. The metrics are assessed separately below.

Interpretation

The score-only policy supports H3 against the three specified baselines. All six intervals lie entirely above zero: the court reduces both risk measures against all three comparators, supporting H3 under the reported bootstrap analysis.

Evidence of skill receives the court’s exact allocation sizes. This control preserves the funded count and total nominal allocation, assigning sizes by training-return rank. Both its CVaR and drawdown comparisons favor the court.

These are comparisons of complete allocation policies. Evidence of skill matches the court’s allocation sizes by construction; deploy-all and the single LLM retain their specified allocation rules. Their mean sizes and funded counts are shown in the allocation context table.

Metrics, uncertainty, and scope

CVaR is mean daily P&L over the worst 5% of days (45 days here), not the single worst day. Less-negative CVaR is better. Maximum drawdown is the largest peak-to-trough decline of cumulative daily P&L, starting from zero. P&L and drawdown are in the source’s book units, not percentage returns. Mean size is the average allocation fraction across the 57 champions; matching it controls nominal allocation, not realized margin or risk on every individual day.

Tables show rounded values; reductions are calculated from unrounded results. Absolute drawdown reduction is baseline minus court. Absolute CVaR loss reduction is court minus baseline because CVaR is reported as signed P&L. Relative reduction divides the absolute reduction by the magnitude of the baseline value.

All intervals were recomputed using 8,000 paired bootstrap samples and identical resampled dates for every policy. P&L/CVaR use iid day resampling; drawdown uses circular moving blocks of 10 days. Seed 0; percentile 95% intervals. CVaR resampling does not preserve serial dependence. Intervals are pairwise and condition on the recorded strategies and LLM decisions.

Scope and provenance: all 58 source champions matched their ledgers; one degraded court panel was excluded from every comparison. The cohort consists of champions, not a documented census of every Pareto-front member. The original report’s unverified arm-A ledger provenance (approximately 37% of the cohort) remains unresolved. These recomputed results use those same inputs and should retain that qualification.
